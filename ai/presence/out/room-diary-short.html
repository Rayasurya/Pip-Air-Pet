How Pip reads the air

  • Someone here. CO2 has stayed at least 80 ppm above the room's empty level for 5 minutes. This is the tested rule.
  • Likely asleep. High, steady CO2 at night. A guess, not a tested result.
  • Air clearing. CO2 is falling. A window opened, or the room emptied. The air alone can't tell which, so Pip makes no call.
  • Empty. CO2 is back near the room's empty level.
  • Pip was off. No readings. Nothing is guessed for that time.

The empty level is the lowest the room's CO2 usually gets. Pip works it out from the last 7 days. Each dot in the drawing is about 14 ppm above it.

"Pip is sure" means the answer stays the same when the limit moves from 60 to 110 ppm. It is not a probability. Pip can't tell who is in the room, or how many people.

This room is simulated, so the page can show what really happened. A real room has no such answer. The simulated sensor is also noisier than the real one, so "likely asleep" never shows here. Those nights read as someone here.
